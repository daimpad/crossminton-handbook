    The footwork system

    Technique · Advanced

    Recommended first: Footwork: center and split step

    Explanation

    open

    In the beginner area you learned the basic cycle: central position, split step, out to the ball, back to the middle. Now you fill this cycle with the individual step types that make each zone of your court reachable fastest. That's the complete footwork system.

    Four step types carry it. The chassé is a sideways shuffle in which the feet never cross — you stay in balance and often use it to prepare the next step. The lunge is the most-used step: a deep, explosive lunge into a corner, hitting-side foot leading, that gets you to the Speeder stable and far. The scissor jump gets you to deep, high Speeders in the rear area — you push off, switch leg position in the air and land ready to hit again. The block jump intercepts fast balls from the side when there's no time for a full step.

    What's decisive is that you choose the right step for each zone — and after every stroke find your way back to the central position with a clean recovery step. The recovery step is the most inconspicuous and the most important: it re-establishes the cycle each time.

    Think of a fencer. Their feet never stand at random — every step has a form and a purpose, and after every attack they return immediately to the base stance. That's exactly the economy you're after for your court.

    At some point this system runs unconsciously. At first you choose the step deliberately; over time your body chooses it itself — and then you're everywhere in time.

    Exercise

    open

    Assign the steps to the zones

    Goal

    Call up the right step type for each zone of the court and recover cleanly.

    How to do it — Part 1

    1. First practice the step types individually, without a Speeder: the chassé sideways, the lunge into the front corners, the scissor jump to the back, the block jump to the side.
    2. With the chassé, make sure the feet never cross.
    3. After each step, return to the central position with a recovery step.

    How to do it — Part 2

    1. Now have Speeders thrown to you into different zones.
    2. Choose the right step for each zone, play the Speeder and return.
    3. Only increase the pace once the step-to-zone assignment is in place.

    Self-check

    Do you choose the right step for each zone, or do you go everywhere with the same steps? And do you reliably find your way back to the central position after every ball? The system only holds once step choice and recovery run smoothly.

    Wrap-up

    The footwork system is the foundation of all advanced strokes — only someone who stands in time and in the right place can play clear, smash and drop cleanly.
