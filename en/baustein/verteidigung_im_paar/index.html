    Defending as a pair

    Tactics · Advanced

    Recommended first: Doubles as its own game

    Explanation

    open

    When the opponent attacks, your pair becomes a defensive unit. Now it's about covering the area, taking the speed out of the attack, and waiting for the moment when you can strike back yourselves.

    The most important thing in defense is that you divide the area sensibly and leave no gap the opponent can hit hard into. Each covers their zone, and the most dangerous space — where the opponent can place their hardest ball — has to be secured. You stand deeper and wider to have time to react.

    In defense you deliberately take the pace out. Instead of hitting the hard ball back hard, you bring it back under control, buy yourself time, and wait for the opponent's mistake or weaker delivery. Every safe return keeps you in the rally and forces the opponent to build the attack up again.

    And you keep a lookout for the switching moment. As soon as the opponent's attack weakens — a shorter smash, a high ball — that's your signal to switch from defense into attack. Defense in doubles is never just warding off; it's waiting for your own chance.

    Together you are a shield that catches the onslaught. A shield with a gap is worthless — the art is for your two halves to interlock seamlessly until the attack exhausts itself.

    Exercise

    open

    Cover the area, take the pace

    Goal

    Catch the attack as a pair, without a gap, and wait for the switching moment.

    How to do it

    1. Have the opponents attack you as a pair (hard, deep balls).
    2. Divide the area clearly, so no gap remains for the hard ball.
    3. Bring the attacking balls back under control instead of answering them hard — take the pace out.
    4. Watch for the moment the attack weakens, and then switch to attack together.
    5. Switch the pairs' attacking and defending roles after a few rounds.

    Self-check

    Does a gap stay between you that the opponent hits hard into? Do you take enough pace out to stay in the rally? And do you recognize the moment you can switch? Good defense covers seamlessly and waits patiently for the chance.

    Wrap-up

    A defense that leaves no gap and uses the switching moment brings you out of trouble and back into attack.
