    Explosiveness and jumping power

    Fitness · Advanced

    Recommended first: Training with purpose, Fast feet

    Explanation

    open

    Crossminton is decided in fractions of a second: the first step to the Speeder, the takeoff for the overhead shot, the sudden change of direction. All of this demands explosiveness — the ability to summon a lot of force in the shortest time.

    Explosiveness is trained through fast, springy movements. The principle behind it uses the natural tension of your muscles: a muscle that is briefly pre-stretched and immediately contracts — as in a springy jump — produces more force than from a standstill. Exactly this spring sits in every good start and takeoff.

    So much for the principle. In the execution, caution is the most important companion. Fast, powerful jumps heavily load tendons and joints. They belong at the end of a solid foundation, not at the beginning, and they demand clean technique — a soft, controlled landing matters more than great height. Whoever increases the load too quickly risks exactly the injury that good training is meant to prevent.

    So the clear advice: the first steps toward jumping power are best taken with qualified guidance that keeps an eye on technique and progression. It shows you which movements suit your level and how to build up safely.

    Explosiveness is like a tensioned spring. Used correctly, it snaps powerfully forward. Overstretched or wrongly loaded, it gives way. The buildup decides which of the two happens.

    Reflection task

    open

    Watch the explosive moments in your game: the first step out of the base position, the takeoff to a high ball, the change of direction. Where do you get away too late or too heavily? That shows you where explosiveness would bring you the most. If you want to work specifically on jumping power, get qualified guidance for it — it ensures safe technique and the right buildup. On your own, watch one thing above all: a soft, controlled landing on everything you jump.
