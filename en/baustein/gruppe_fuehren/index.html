    Leading a group

    Coaching · Coach view

    Recommended first: What good teaching takes

    Explanation

    open

    As soon as you work with more than one person, organization joins the teaching. To lead a group means to form a sensible session out of different people, limited space, and limited time.

    Four things you bring together in this. First the different levels. In almost every group beginners stand next to more advanced players. Instead of having everyone do the same thing, you give the same exercise with different demands — one plays to a big target, another to a small one. That way no one is under-challenged and no one left behind.

    Second the dramaturgy of the session. A good training session has an arc: a warm-up that prepares and creates appetite; a main part with a clear focus; a wind-down, often free play that applies the focus. Whoever plans without this arc only strings exercises together.

    Third space and material. In Crossminton this is rewarding — without a net and with a few markings you quickly set up several courts. Make sure everyone is moving and no one waits long. Long waiting lines are the most common mistake and the surest way to lose motivation.

    Fourth motivation and safety. Keep the mood positive and the demands reachable, watch for enough distance between courts and for safe handling of the equipment. People learn better when they feel safe and have fun.

    You are the host, not the overseer. A good host makes sure everyone is occupied, feels comfortable, and gladly comes back in the end.

    Reflection task

    open

    Plan a short training session for a mixed group: sketch a warm-up, a main part with a single focus, and a wind-down. For the main part, consider how you make the same exercise differently demanding for stronger and weaker players, and how you divide the space so no one waits long. This planning in advance is the difference between a led session and a mere stringing-together.
