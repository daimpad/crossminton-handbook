    Deception — hiding your intent

    Technique · Expert

    Recommended first: The short game: the drop shot, Slice and spin

    Explanation

    open

    At expert level it is more and more often the hidden intention that decides the point, no longer the better stroke alone. Deception is the expert's signature: the opponent reads your stroke wrong because you don't reveal what's coming until the last moment.

    The secret lies in the preparation that always looks the same. Whether you hit hard, drop short, or play long — your approach, your backswing, your body posture are identical. Only in the very last moment before contact do you decide which stroke it becomes. The feint sits on a sequence you already master; you only add the late decision.

    Timing is everything. You hold the stroke until the last instant and then strike — deciding too early gives you away, too late ruins the stroke. On the disguised drop shot, you first swing quickly as if for a hard stroke and only take the pace out just before contact.

    Your feet tell the story too. When you fake a smash, your approach has to fit the smash — a half-hearted movement exposes the feint immediately. And after the deception you return to balance at once, because a good opponent gets to a deceived ball too.

    Deception is a poker face. Your cards change, your face stays the same. Only when it's too late does the opponent see what you're really holding.

    One more principle: use it sparingly. A feint the opponent has already seen three times is no feint anymore. Deception lives on surprise, not on repetition.

    Exercise

    open

    Same preparation, late decision

    Goal

    Play the clear, drop shot, and hard stroke from an identical preparation and decide only late.

    How to do it — Part 1

    1. From the back court, play the clear, drop shot, and drive one after another — with deliberately identical preparation: same approach, same backswing, even on the soft drop shot.
    2. At first exaggerate the full preparation on the drop shot too, until it feels the same as on the hard stroke.

    How to do it — Part 2

    1. Have a partner feed you and decide only at the last moment which of the three strokes you play.
    2. On the deceived drop shot: swing quickly as if for a hard stroke, take the pace out only just before contact.
    3. Return to balance immediately after every stroke.

    Self-check

    Ask your partner whether they see your stroke coming early. Does your preparation already give away what's coming? Does the soft drop shot look exactly like the hard stroke — until the last moment? Only a truly neutral preparation deceives.

    Wrap-up

    Deception is the core of the expert game. It stands and falls with the neutral preparation — and with the restraint to show it only at the right moment.
